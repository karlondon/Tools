2 Blocker Issues — Must Resolve Before Day 1

🚫 BLOCKER 1

Security rules have wrong zone assignments — traffic will be silently blocked after Day 1

The Missing routes on NE Palo Alto.xlsx file identifies 17 security rules where the source zone is DMZFE (untrust/eth1/1) but traffic will arrive on DMZBE (trust/eth1/2) after the Day 1 route change — or vice versa. PAN-OS matches security rules on zone, not interface. After Day 1, traffic arrives on eth1/2 (DMZBE) but the rules expect DMZFE — no rule matches → default deny → silent drop.

⚡ This is the single biggest risk. Implementing Day 1 routes WITHOUT fixing zones first will cause widespread connectivity failures that look like routing issues but are actually policy drops at the NGFW.

🚫 BLOCKER 2

NE NGFW has zero IPSec tunnels — 10.0.0.0/8 supernet route will expose the gap

EOIA Day 1 adds 10.0.0.0/8 → 10.36.128.33 (eth1/2) as a supernet catch-all. This is correct design — but in the current state, both NGFWs have zero IPSec tunnels (all removed by the project revert). The route will be installed, traffic from all 10.x.x.x sources will arrive on eth1/2 — but the NGFW has nowhere to send branch traffic. All UK, IE, SP/PT, DE/GE branch site traffic will hit the NGFW and be dropped (no tunnel interfaces, no branch static routes).

The existing situation (branch traffic drops) will not be made worse by Day 1, but it will not be fixed either. More critically, branch-originated traffic that was previously being silently dropped at the NGFW will now arrive on the correct interface (eth1/2/DMZBE) but still be dropped — and the security policy zone checks (post-zone-fix) may generate new deny logs that are misleading.

⚠️
Recommended pre-condition: Restore IPSec tunnel configuration from Panorama pre-project backup before Day 1. Without this, the EOIA monitoring gap (48hrs between Day 1 and Day 2) will show branch replication still broken — which may block the Day 2 go/no-go decision. If Panorama restore cannot be completed before Day 1, the team must accept that branch site connectivity will remain broken through the EOIA window and document this explicitly as a known exception.

3 High-Risk Issues

⚠️ HIGH 1

Hub DC replication (WE↔NE) will genuinely break at Day 1 step 1.8 unless WE route is pre-added to NE NGFW

Currently, WE DC ↔ NE DC traffic bypasses the NGFW via VNet Global Peering (peering route /22 beats UDR /16). After EOIA Day 1 step 1.8, the Azure GATEWAY2025 route table gains explicit /32 host routes for DC3 (10.36.128.70/32) and DC4 (10.36.128.71/32) pointing to eth1/2 (10.36.128.38). Traffic arriving FROM on-prem/ExpressRoute destined to DC03/DC04 will now correctly reach the NGFW on eth1/2. However, the return traffic from DC03/DC04 destined to WE DCs (10.36.0.x) will hit the NGFW which still has no route for 10.36.0.0/17 (WE VNet).

The RFC schedule shows Day 1 was 12 May 2026 and Day 2 is 14 May 2026 (today). The phased plan requires a 48-hour monitoring gap between Day 1 and Day 2 to validate routing changes. ⚠️ HIGH 3

AD replication monitoring during the EOIA gaps will be unreliable given existing stale 1722 state

Both monitoring gaps (G1 and G2) require checking repadmin /replsummary for AD replication health as a go/no-go gate. However, as established in the routing analysis:

  • Hub DC 1722 errors are stale — they reflect historical failures, not current state
  • Branch DC 1722 errors are genuine and ongoing — and will remain until IPSec tunnels are restored
  • repadmin /replsummary will show failures regardless of whether the EOIA changes are causing them
💡
Recommended monitoring approach during EOIA gaps:
Use repadmin /showrepl /csv output timestamped at the point of the change as baseline. Any 1722 errors with last-attempt timestamps after Day 1 completion time indicate EOIA-caused failures. Pre-existing errors (timestamps before Day 1) should be documented as known pre-existing and excluded from go/no-go gates. Supplement with dcdiag /test:replications for live connectivity assessment.

4 Medium-Risk Issues

🟡 MEDIUM 1

ADDS-To-ADDS-Global_UKI_In/Out rules have 400M+ hits — zone change will interrupt active UKI replication

Rules ADDS-To-ADDS-Global_UKI_In (485M hits) and ADDS-To-ADDS-Global_UKI_Out (77M hits) are actively used for UKI ↔ NE DC communication. After Day 1, UKI traffic arrives on eth1/2 (DMZBE) but these rules currently have DMZFE as source zone. Zone fix must be applied before Day 1 or these will break during the implementation window. The zone change itself will briefly interrupt active sessions (PA policy commits cause session clear for affected flows).

⚠️ Schedule the zone fix commit during a low-traffic window. Run repadmin /syncall immediately after to force replication recovery. Inform SOC of expected brief traffic interruption.
🟡 MEDIUM 2

No-NAT-Internal rule (Day 2) references only /27 subnets — broader internal sources may still be NATted

The Day 2 No-NAT-Internal rule specifies source as 10.36.128.64/27 (AD subnet) and 10.36.128.96/27 (PAW subnet). After Day 1, traffic from ALL internal 10.x.x.x sources routes via eth1/2. If any internal source outside those two /27s has internet-destined traffic that also needs to reach internal destinations, the Internet_NAT rule below will match first for those sources — creating incorrect source NAT on internal sessions.

⚠️ Consider broadening the No-NAT-Internal source to 10.0.0.0/8 to cover all internal RFC1918 sources, not just the two hub subnets. Validate that this doesn't affect legitimate internet NAT for hub VMs.

5 Safe / Low-Risk Items (proceed as planned)

✅ SAFE 1

DMZBE NSG changes (Step 1.1) — correct, critical, apply first

The RFC correctly identifies Allow-OnPrem-VPN-Inbound and Allow-OnPrem-VPN-Outbound rules on OCG-NE-SHUB-NSG-DMZBE-01 as CRITICAL pre-requisites. These NSG changes are non-disruptive before route changes and correctly ordered in the plan. No issues with this step.

✅ SAFE 2

Day 3 security profile groups — safe, no connectivity impact

Creating new profile groups (Strict-Internet-Profile and Alert-Internal-VPN-Profile) and assigning them to policies changes inspection behaviour only — not routing or zone matching. The main profile change switches from BLOCK to ALERT mode for internal/VPN traffic, which reduces the risk of false-positive drops. This is a well-designed change and safe to proceed once Day 1 and Day 2 are validated.

✅ SAFE 3

Non-RFC1918 static routes (SonyFTP, AFTP, UKI ExpressRoute, Visolit) — low risk additions

The specific host/public IP routes added in Day 1 step 1.5 (173.251.241.30/32, 173.0.93.197/32, 213.205.135.238/32, 62.232.203.118/32, 84.17.200.0/22) are more specific than the default route and simply redirect these specific destinations via eth1/2 instead of eth1/1. These will have no impact until corresponding security rules match — and those rules (ANSDC-Sony-Outbound, AFTP-Outbound-Updated) already have correct DMZBE→DMZFE zone assignments.
